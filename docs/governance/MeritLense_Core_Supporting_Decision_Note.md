## Classification decision

Preparation, reporting, consent, hand hygiene, equipment checks and final checks cannot be declared Supporting just because of their category. Ask whether the evidence is substantive to the actual question and the role. Conversely, not every mention of safety, reporting or PPE is a compulsory gate: preserve the condition and task context.

Core means an assessed task outcome or substantive safeguard. It does not itself mean Mandatory, an automatic zero, a global critical failure, or certificate refusal. A non-compensable prerequisite and its omission consequence require a separate approved question-level rule.

Supporting means elaboration or context that does not independently establish a central outcome. Some supporting omissions can distinguish complete from partial performance where the approved question rubric explicitly says so. Do not impose an unseen requirement or infer an affirmative unsafe act from an omission.

Combined cells need atomic evidence mapping where their components have different consequences. For example, “final check and hand hygiene” cannot justify treating hand hygiene as optional, or making a cosmetic final glance an automatic safety gate. Preserve source IDs until a versioned split is agreed.

## Scoring decision for adoption

Reject the generic count shortcut of exactly one Core omission = 3 and two Core omissions = 0. The supporting/core split alone does not close the partial-credit gap.

For standard 5/3/0 questions, encode these predicates per question:

1. 5: all applicable essential outcomes and the approved full-credit conditions are evidenced. Accept equivalent safe methods and concise wording.
2. 3: the approved minimum viable competence is evidenced safely, with one or more permitted compensable gaps. No separately approved prerequisite for partial credit is failed.
3. 0: the response fails that question’s minimum viable competence, or an explicit approved zero-score condition is supported by reliable evidence.

Each question still needs its actual essential-outcome IDs, minimum partial-credit condition, any non-compensable prerequisite, and approved negative-evidence consequences. These are professional scoring specifications, not engineering guesses. This package does not invent them for unreviewed rows.

Do not turn every Core omission into automatic Not Ready. A question score, competency threshold and global safety gate are separate states. Keep 70 legacy exceptions, published maxima and existing readiness/certificate rules unchanged unless a specific revision is explicitly adopted.

## Test and deployment boundary

Activate only explicitly adopted question/rule versions whose complete predicates pass the following tests: full evidence; permitted partial evidence; omission of a prerequisite; omission of supporting detail; conditional inapplicability; equivalent paraphrase; affirmed versus negated unsafe action; and platform evidence loss. Verify EN/AR and representative other supported translation paths against those same expected outcomes.

Use approved equivalent questions within the same role and required coverage if a particular rubric is not ready. Do not reduce coverage or apply an unresolved interim zero. Routine incomplete answers are scored automatically under the completed rule; they do not go to an administrator because the rubric was never finished.

Re-score only identified pending test sessions with a new approved scoring run and preserve the original evidence, scores and version. Do not overwrite published historical outcomes. Recalculation is not automatic release or automatic certificate eligibility; run the independent completion, readiness, safety and certificate checks. Valid reports and captured transcripts remain accessible where unaffected.
